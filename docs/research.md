# Research basis and verification record

Verified on 2026-11-25 against original papers, publisher pages, and author/university copies. These are conceptual references, not a psychological assessment battery. No questionnaire items, scoring keys, paper PDFs, or private datasets are redistributed. The project's Action → Feedback → Reward → Friction scaffold is an engineering heuristic, not a validated unified theory.

| Richard M. Ryan, C. Scott Rigby & Andrew Przybylski (2006), *The Motivational Pull of Video Games: A Self-Determination Theory Approach*. DOI [10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11031-006-9051-8). [Author-hosted full text](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2006_RyanRigbyPrzybylski_MandE.pdf). | Four studies: laboratory samples reported as 89, 50 and 58 undergraduates, then an online MMO-community survey of 730. Lab studies used gameplay sessions, self-reports and repeated-game comparisons. | Need satisfaction was associated with enjoyment and future-play preferences. Autonomy, competence and relatedness are reference constructs; game ownership cannot measure them. Short lab exposure and selected samples limit generalization. Study 3's reported attendance counts do not sum cleanly to its stated sample; do not silently recalculate a pooled N. |
| Vero Vanden Abeele, Katta Spiel, Lennart Nacke, Daniel Johnson & Kathrin Gerling (2020), *Development and validation of the player experience inventory: A scale to measure player experiences at the level of functional and psychosocial consequences*. IJHCS 135, 102370. DOI [10.1016/j.ijhcs.2019.102370](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2019.102370). | Five development/validation studies, 64 games-user-research experts and 529 players. Construct validity, convergent/discriminant evidence and invariance analyses were examined. | PXI distinguishes functional consequences (such as progress feedback and controls) from psychosocial consequences (such as mastery and curiosity). This helps avoid equating a mechanic with a motive. Validation of a self-report instrument does not validate this agent's inferences or establish a particular person's causal reward loop. |
| Nick Ballou, Alena Denisova, Richard Ryan, C. Scott Rigby & Sebastian Deterding (2024), *The Basic Needs in Games Scale (BANGS): A new tool for investigating positive and negative video game experiences*. IJHCS 188, 103289. DOI [10.1016/j.ijhcs.2024.103289](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2024.103289). [University-hosted full text](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/212508/1/1-s2.0-S1071581924000739-main.pdf). | Five studies; 1,246 unique participants. EFA/CFA, validity, measurement invariance and longitudinal analyses. The table includes 1,891 repeated observations from 414 Xbox participants; these are not 1,891 unique people. | Satisfaction and frustration of the three needs are distinct; relatedness can extend beyond multiplayer. Autonomy-frustration reliability was weaker and the relatedness satisfaction/frustration relationship was unexpectedly nonsignificant. Do not translate hours into need-satisfaction scores. |

Full item-level methods remain unverified. |

DOI [10.1177/15554120241242332](https://doi.org/10.1177/15554120241242332). First published online April 13, 2024. | A vocabulary for diverse reasons to play and gaps in existing models. Do not turn dimensions into fixed player/personality types or numeric individual scores. Online year differs from the later volume year; both are explicitly recorded. |

Reasoning rules: separate theory, self-report constructs, verified mechanics, direct user evidence and model hypotheses. Consider competing explanations and contextual exceptions. Correlation is not causation. A group result does not measure an individual. Curiosity and meaning are useful concepts but are not additional SDT basic needs. Recency of publication is not evidence of quality. Never reproduce or administer protected scale questions without checking authorization. Revisit primary research only when a specific new question warrants it; ordinary recommendations reuse these constraints.
